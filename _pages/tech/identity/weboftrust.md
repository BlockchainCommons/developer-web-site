_A web of trust is an organically linked group of identities that can
help to measure the trust of a specific endorsement._

## Why Are Webs of Trust Important?

_Who do you trust?_ That's the question when you're assessing an
[endorsement](/identity/attestations/). Certainly, the [fair witness
methodology](/identity/fairwitness/) can help, but you still have to
have trust that the attestor is using it fairly, impartially, and
correctly. At some point, there needs to be some root ability to trust
someone, even if their identity is [entirely
pseudonymous](/identity/ppp/).

That root trust can come from a web of trust. Though an endorsement
from someone you don't know might have some faint value, an
endorsement from someone you already know and trust will have much
greater value. And, if a number of people you know all endorse a new
identity, that "web of trust" will have even more value for you.

Though they create a web of trust, these are "peer-to-peer
interactions": a singular person endorses another. They are peers
because neither is in a position of authority; instead the trust is
decentralized. Only together do these singular endorsements begin to
create a web.

Ultimately, this requires pseudonymous identity systems to build
networking as a vital part of their system, even if it's just the
ability for one identity to sign endorsements about another identity.

## How Do Webs of Trust Work?

The creation of Webs of Trust is simple: you create a way for one
identifier to provably link to another identifier. Blockchain Commons'
[XIDs](/xid/) do this with edges: one identifier is used to create and
sign an attestation about another identifier, then the other
identifier can choose to link it into their XID, which they can then
sign before distribution. The simple act of signing keys with keys was
the mechanic in PGP, the technology that founded the idea of a web of
trust. Many other options are possibly, but the important thing is for
links to be signed, so that they're provable.

Blockchain Commons doesn't currently have a methodology for assessing
the level of trust generated by a web of trust. The traditional method
is to give each trust link a value, and for that value to be higher if
the attestor is directly linked to you and lower the further away they
get. Summing up trust values from many different attestors can then
reveal your level of personal trust, based on your web of connections.
